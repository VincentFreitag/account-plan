01 · Discovery

The territory, and how I know it

Digital-native business means selling to companies whose product is software — AI-native startups, marketplaces, scaleups. The buying pattern there is unlike anything in my current territory. It is, however, exactly the way I buy.

Economic buyerTechnical founders who read the API docs themselves
AdoptionDeveloper-led, bottom-up — in production before procurement hears of it
CycleWeeks, not quarters
CommercialsConsumption — the account expands with usage, not with contract anniversaries

I know this buyer because I have been this buyer for two years: a Claude subscription that became a thinking partner, then Claude Code, then an agent stack that runs my ventures’ knowledge management. Chat → code → agents is not a diagram to me; it is the expansion path I personally travelled — and the one this seat sells along. The rest of this page is the evidence.

04 · Objections

The four I would raise against me

Handled the way I would handle them on a call: named plainly, answered honestly.

Objection 01“The ad asks for 5+ years of enterprise sales. You’re at four and a quarter.”

Right — 4 years and 3 months carrying a number at Signify, and I won’t round it up. What I would point at instead: inside that time I built a business model and opened a €130M segment — work that usually appears much later in a sales career. If the bar is literally five years of quota, I am short by two quarters. If the bar is what the years produced, I am ahead of it.

Objection 02“You sell LED modules. This job sells software, priced on consumption.”

What transfers: technical buying committees, qualification against multifaceted requirements, long education-led cycles — four years of teaching buyers technology they did not know they needed. What does not transfer: consumption commercials. I name that openly as my fastest required ramp, and I would want a manager who holds me to it — not a candidate story pretending hardware and tokens are the same sale.

Objection 03“Your customers are industrial OEMs. This segment’s customers are digital natives.”

True — and it is why I am applying to this segment and not another. I do not just understand this buyer; I am this buyer: two years on Claude, products shipped, an agent stack in daily use. When a technical founder explains what they are trying to build, I will not be translating from a persona document.

Objection 04“You build companies on the side. Are you actually a seller — and will you stay one?”

The record says seller: quota exceeded six consecutive quarters, €10M+ captured. Building is how I sell technical products credibly — I can demo what I understand because I have made one. The ventures are the reason I am in this conversation, not my exit from it.

05 · Close

Why this company, and the ask

This month I passed two years as a Claude subscriber. In that time it became my thinking partner for the business models I build at Signify, and the reason Joyzy.me exists — an end-to-end product I could not have shipped on my own.

That path led to the knowledge system in stage 03, and to its central rule: no model writes to it directly. I built the rule after a model, mid-project, wrote two conflicting versions of the same fact — neither flagged as uncertain. That day settled what I now believe is the real buying criterion for this technology: not what a model can do, but what it can be trusted to do unsupervised. As agents take over more of the execution, the model’s character and its safeguards are the product.

I have spent two years deciding which model to trust with my own work, and I keep landing on the same one.

I want this seat because I have been my own customer for two years — and I want companies to get from this technology what I got from it.

If we speak, I’ll bring the discovery questions I would ask your digital-native customers in my first 30 days.
